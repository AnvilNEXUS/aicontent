The Dilemma of All-or-Nothing Assessment
vs. Sovereign BBS+ Disclosure

In legacy credentialing, learners are confronted with an impossible trade-off: either surrender their private drafts, collaborative dialogues, and unpolished iterations to an institutional cloud silo, or keep their work entirely private in an offline vault where it commands zero external social or professional trust.

The Legacy All-or-Nothing Trap

Submitting a portfolio forces the learner to reveal 100% of their notes, private corrections, and mentor debates to every third-party verifier. Alternatively, standard W3C RSA/ECDSA credentials sign the entire payload as one blob—if you redact a single field, the digital signature breaks.

Failure Mode: Verifiers track and link the learner across multiple websites via constant, static public keys and unblinded signatures.
The PELICAN + BBS+ Solution

The PELICAN Protocol produces an 8-claim vector dossier. Using BLS12-381 BBS+ Cryptography, a single mentor signature signs all 8 messages simultaneously. The learner can derive mathematically provable presentations that reveal any subset of claims while completely masking others.

Sovereignty Guarantee: Zero-Knowledge Proofs randomize the signature upon every presentation, guaranteeing complete cryptographic unlinkability across relying parties.

Bounded Commitments & Architectural Guardrails

          

          

            In SOVRAEGIS, cryptography enforces procedural fairness and authentic attribution—it does not substitute for sound pedagogical judgment.
          

        


        

          
          

            

              
              1. Bounded Tamper-Evidence
            

            
Attribution vs. Absolute Truth

            

              A cryptographic BBS+ signature mathematically proves who made what statement about which version of a SYNCTESS. It does not magically prove that the mentor’s pedagogical opinion was infallibly correct, nor that the underlying scientific theory will never be falsified.
            

          


          

            

              
              2. Balanced Sovereignty
            

            
Selective Disclosure vs. Forgery

            

              The learner possesses complete sovereignty over which claims to disclose or conceal. However, the learner cannot forge or modify a reviewer’s statement. If a claim is revealed, it must match the mentor’s signed attestation bit-for-bit, preserving reciprocal accountability.
            

          


          

            

              
              3. Decoupled Credentialing
            

            
Attestation vs. SYNTHCRED Issuance

            

              Completing a PELICAN review does not automatically mint a credential. It outputs an evidentiary package containing verified findings. The determination to synthesize a formal SYNTHCRED remains the exclusive authority of an accredited Guild or institution.
