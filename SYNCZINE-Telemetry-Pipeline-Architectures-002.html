1 The "Video Player Analogy" & Singling Out

Omitting a learner's DID does not make telemetry anonymous. Under the EDPB 01/2025 guidelines and Vietnam’s Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 91/2025/QH15), data protection applies to any stream that can single out a person. If a system records that "Person X paused 3 times at 142 seconds, backtracked to SYNCZ-02, and spent 18 minutes", that unique fingerprint isolates their behavioral trajectory just as effectively as a username.

2 Timing Side-Channels with Verifiable Credentials

When learners complete a SYNCZINE, they frequently issue or export a cryptographic DID-signed Credential or submit assignments to an LMS. If telemetry packets arrive at server timestamp 15:42:11 UTC and the DID credential is cryptographically anchored at 15:42:14 UTC, any observer with access to server network ingress can trivially correlate the telemetry instance UUID back to the learner's public identity.

3 Small-Cohort Deduction Vulnerability

In tutoring groups or small classroom cohorts of 3 to 8 learners, "anonymized" timestamps are futile. An educator who knows Learner Alice was struggling while Learner Bob finished early can review individual telemetry curves on an analytics dashboard and deduce precisely which run belonged to whom, circumventing privacy protections.

4 The Edge-Computation & k-Anonymity Guardrail

Mode 3 solves these vulnerabilities simultaneously:
1. On-device synthesis: Raw timestamps are converted locally to relative velocity scores and friction ratios against baseline manifests.
2. Jitter delay: A random 30-90 second transmission offset detaches egress packet arrival from credential signing.
3. k-Anonymity ($k \ge 10$): The curriculum dashboard prohibits viewing any SYNCZ heatmap until at least 10 unique runs have aggregated.
