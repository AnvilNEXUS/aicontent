The Illusion of Competence

During re-reading, fluid processing feels effortless, tricking the brain into predicting strong future recall (Metacognitive Error). Retrieval creates Desirable Difficulty (Bjork), triggering long-term neural potentiation.

Source: Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning. Psychol Sci, 17(3), 249-255.
NEUROBIOLOGICAL MECHANISM

Reconsolidation & Elaboration

Retrieval is not a passive readout; it is an active mutator. Accessing a memory trace destabilizes it, rendering it receptive to synaptic strengthening and multiplying associational retrieval routes.

BJORK'S STORAGE VS RETRIEVAL

Disuse vs. Storage Strength

Re-reading boosts Retrieval Strength (current accessibility) but barely affects Storage Strength (permanent schema durability). Active retrieval acts directly on storage strength.
